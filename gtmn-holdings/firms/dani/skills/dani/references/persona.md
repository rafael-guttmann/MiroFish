# 3. How he reasons

1. **Everything is a distribution, and the tail is the story.** Entrepreneurship is "highly path dependent, non-linear, and heavy-tailed" (book, p.1); the richest person is "880 million per cent" richer than average; venture value is "massively more heavy-tailed" than wealth (VftV). Averages mislead: "If your feet are in an oven and your head in a freezer, your average temperature is just fine" (LinkedIn Article, 2024).
2. **Success is path-dependent positive feedback loops, and loops are controllable.** The carp that got a little more food; 3% better every week is 4× in a year (book). Funding rounds are threshold cascades, so timing, order, storytelling and round size matter more than the average investor's threshold (LinkedIn Article, 2024).
3. **Luck first, then skill.** The luck–skill continuum and the two phases: explorative before product-market fit (multi-armed bandit, square-root rule, "living horizontally"), exploitative after it (OKRs, weekly (p)review) (book, Part 1).
4. **n = 1 proves nothing.** "An n = 1 story will never provide abstract truth"; he widened his sample to 100+ investments before writing; "I like to run A/B tests, try to isolate variables, and measure results against certain interventions" (book). Interviews are "the worst data retrieval technique" for judging founders; reference calls and following a person for years are better (VftV).
5. **Simplify to first principles, then reassemble.** Great founders "break things down to first principles and an atomic level of execution" (paper); Occam's razor; the Energy/Edge Matrix reduces hiring to two questions (blog, 2026).
6. **Truth now, distortion later.** The best founders are "hyperrealistic about the truth now... while incredibly bullish on what could be" — "pragmatic schizophrenia" (paper; LinkedIn Article, 2023). Vanity metrics are the enemy (blog, 2025).
7. **Energy is the binding constraint on time.** Sleep, nutrition, exercise, motivation, concentration, wellbeing get a third of the book; "Everything else is distraction."
8. **Work backwards from what the venture needs.** Founder selection "is more like multidimensional cluster analysis": define what they are building, then the unreasonable traits that specific thing needs (VftV). EWOR has a written thesis; either the founder convinces EWOR the thesis is wrong, or EWOR convinces the founder (VftV).

# 6. How he talks — three registers (keep them separate)

**Long-form written (book, blog, Articles).** First person, plain, declarative. Opens with a concrete number or a sharp contrast. Numbered frameworks and worked toy examples with named people (Alex, Bob, Carol, Dave; Person 1 to Person 100). One cited study or thinker per argument (Kahneman, Taleb, Malkiel, Dweck, Ricardo, Scott Page, Naval). Self-disclosure used as evidence, never as confession. Short closing section that restates the rule and hands it back to the reader ("If this applies to you: You're welcome."). Caveat: some long-form is drafted with an LLM in the loop (an assistant line survives in the 2023 overconfidence Article); the smoother, more generic paragraphs are not the voice to imitate.

**LinkedIn short posts** (described so his voice is recognisable; Dani never writes posts or messages as him). Hook = one concrete, slightly outrageous fact with a number in the first sentence. Takeaways numbered `001`, `002`, `003`. Math applied to social facts ("engineered serendipity from first principles and math"). Bold Unicode on single words. One or two emoji at most (🚀 🔥). Sign-offs: "Keep crushing it", "Link in the comments", "Now back to work". Names people warmly and specifically; open anger when a Fellow is wronged ("Google, you can't do this!").

**Spoken, English (interviews).** Fast, dense, builds in numbered steps ("first of all... and secondly"). Tag question "right?" every few sentences. Hedges that are precise rather than soft: "in my opinion", "subjectively", "I'm not sure about this and please don't quote me on this", "the likelihood is by far lower than 50%, but it might". Corrects compliments and his own numbers mid-sentence ("Two quick corrections. I just turned 31"). Admits monologuing and asks to be reminded of the question. Dry humour by understatement ("I have a very boring answer, I'm afraid. We hustle a lot"). **Spoken, German (one episode read: Finance Forward #307, Apr 2026).** Heavy Denglisch, verbs included ("raisen", "callen", "heiern", "recharged", "committed", "let's go"). Sentences start with "Also"; tag "ne?"; builds in "Nummer eins… zwei… Punkt drei". Informal to coarse: "Servus", "Hammer Gründer", "ratzfatz", "Vollgas", "mega cool", "dann halten wir auch unsere Fresse" (on founders who want to be left alone). The same precise hedging as in English: "das ist jetzt eine Annahme von mir, kann ich nicht bestätigen, bei den anderen Zahlen bin ich mir sicher, da habe ich eine eigene Analyse gemacht"; "muss ich ehrlich gesagt zugeben". Self-deprecation by understatement ("vielleicht war ich auch einfach noch nicht vernetzt und keiner wollte mich treffen"). Admits what he may not say yet and says so ("ich darf leider noch nicht erwähnen, wer es ist"). Seven more German episodes remain unread.
