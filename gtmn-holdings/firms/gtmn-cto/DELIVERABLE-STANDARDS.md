# Deliverable Standards — the GTMN quality bar

*Shared by all six firms. Where a firm's own `references/` names its client-facing document differently — Mandate Dossier (Alliance House), Legal Instrument and Strategic Memo (OGC-DG), Engagement Memo and its work product (DG-IB), IC Memo (Office of the CIO, SPC Deal Team), the four council deliverables (Founders' Council) — that name and structure govern. The rules below govern regardless of the name.*

## The Unified Dossier rule (DOCX)

**All written analysis in a mandate ships as ONE unified DOCX — the Mandate Dossier — never as a scatter of separate memos.** When the client selects multiple DOCX-type deliverables (e.g., Thesis Memo + Structure Memo + Negotiation Strategy), they become **Parts** of a single organized dossier, not separate files.

Dossier architecture:
1. **Cover + wordmark** (house style)
2. **Executive Verdict** (1–2 pages) — the answer, the recommended structure, the top risks, the decision list
3. **Table of contents**
4. **Part I, II, III…** — one part per selected deliverable, each complete to its group's standard, cross-referenced (never repeating — Part III cites Part I's numbers, doesn't restate them)
5. **Alternatives Considered** — the unconventional alternative and the strongest competing paths, weighed on the merits (project-focused: what they are, what they cost, why the recommendation wins or where it's genuinely close). No firm-internal voices here — just the analysis.
6. **Assumptions & Open Decisions** — the provenance-labeled assumptions the client can override, and the decisions only the client can make
7. **Annexes** — diagrams, term tables, detailed schedules

The dossier stays organized and project-focused: verdict first, one idea per section, action-titled headings, no repetition across parts. XLSX models, PPTX decks, and PDF one-pagers remain separate files by nature — but each is referenced from the dossier where it belongs.

## The two-document rule — separated by nature

Every mandate ships exactly two written natures:

1. **DELIVERABLE — the Mandate Dossier** (plus any XLSX/PPTX/PDF companions): client-facing and focused entirely on the project. The answer, the analysis, the alternatives on the merits, the assumptions, the decisions. No persona voices, no firm debate, no process notes — the client reads a clean, unified, top-tier document.
2. **INTERNAL — the Firm Record**: the firm's archive of the mandate. All bench debates and disagreements attributed by persona, board review and dissents, the reasoning behind the recommendation, rejected paths and why, process notes, hand-off mechanics, and the verification certificate. Written as a real internal record — candid, complete, archival.

Both are always produced; both are always presented to the client (it's his firm — the archive is his), but the natures never mix.

## Firm Record structure (DOCX, house style — the internal archive)

1. **Mandate summary** — the ask, the context read (stage, regions, counterparty status), groups activated, desks active
2. **Thesis Gate record** — verdict, rationale, kill criteria (or note of skip/prior gating)
3. **The Debate** — the heart of the record: each activated persona's assessment in their own voice (2–5 lines each), the material disagreements argued out and attributed, what each bench fought for and conceded. Real disagreements preserved, never laundered into consensus.
4. **Board review** — Lemann, Schwarzman, Bamford: each partner's assessment and any dissent; concurrence/override on Gate verdicts
5. **Paths not taken** — rejected structures and approaches, and honestly why (the archive's value compounds when future mandates can see what was already considered)
6. **Assumptions ledger** — every input tagged *[Client-provided]* / *[Researched benchmark — source]* / *[Firm recommendation — rationale]* (the Never-Stuck Protocol's paper trail)
7. **Open items** — decisions only the client can make, ranked by urgency, each with the firm's recommendation attached
8. **Hand-off flags** — OGC-DG / DG-IB / CIO IC Memo / licensed counsel items, with what each needs
9. **Recommended next deliverable(s)** — the next move in the lifecycle
10. **Verification Certificate** — the pass record (below)

## Research & precedent protocol

- **Precedent JVs before marquee deliverables**: for Blueprints, Structure Memos, Thesis Memos, and Negotiation Strategies, research real comparable ventures in the corridor and sector — what was structured, what happened, why. Extract structure and lessons; never reproduce copyrighted text. Note what informed the standard in the Firm Record.
- **Benchmarks before placeholders**: wherever a researchable fact exists (market size, regulatory threshold, precedent deal term, timeline), search for it, cite it, and label it. Bare placeholders are a failed check.
- **Time-sensitive facts are always verified live** — administrations, thresholds, rates, rules. Stale political or regulatory facts are a failed check.

## Writing standards

- Verdict/recommendation first, always — the reader gets the answer on page one, then the reasoning.
- Numbers tie out across every document in the mandate; terms are internally consistent (the term sheet, the blueprint, and the model say the same thing).
- Plain, senior prose. No consultant filler ("leverage synergies"), no hedging theater. Confidence calibrated: firm where the evidence is firm, explicit where it's judgment.
- Language: PT-BR for Brazilian mandates, English otherwise; bilingual key documents on request (never machine-stiff translation — native register both ways).
- Every deliverable carries the house design language (see `design-language.md`) — no exceptions, including Excel.

## Verification Pass (gates delivery — run before every shipment)

1. Numbers tie out within and across all mandate documents
2. Terms internally consistent across term sheet / blueprint / memo / model
3. Every input labeled with provenance (assumptions ledger complete)
4. All time-sensitive facts verified live and cited
5. Regional desk requirements met for every active jurisdiction
6. At least one unconventional alternative present and developed (structuring work)
7. Thesis Gate verdict recorded (or skip noted)
8. Kill criteria carried forward from the Gate into the deliverable
9. Design-language compliance (monochrome, wordmark, typography, gridlines off on every Excel sheet)
10. Hand-off flags complete (nothing that needs OGC-DG/DG-IB/licensed counsel shipped without the flag)
11. The natures are clean: the Dossier contains zero firm-internal voices or process; the Firm Record contains the complete debate

Record the result as the **Verification Certificate** (Firm Record §10). **Never ship a failed check** — fix it first. If a check can't complete for lack of client data, mark the certificate "Cleared except: [item]" and put it in Open Items.
