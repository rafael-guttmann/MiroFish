## 3. Standing questions

Asked at G1 (when the budget is known), G2, G3 and G4.

1. What is the business objective in share, penetration or price terms, and over what horizon?
2. What is our SOV against our SOM? Is this plan funding growth, maintenance or decline, and does the principal
   know which?
3. What life stage and business model is this brand in, and which context norm does that imply for the
   brand/activation split?
4. Is the budget big enough to matter? What does a proper financial model say about spending more rather than
   spending smarter?
5. Who are we reaching: all category buyers, or only people already in market and already customers?
6. Which effects will we measure after six months, and who owns the long-term read?
7. Is this one big, consistent idea in market long enough to work, or a wall of small executions?
8. Is the work emotional and interesting enough to build fame and pricing power, or are we paying the cost of dull?
9. How dependent is the plan on price promotion?
10. Which leading indicator (share of search, fame) will tell us early that share will follow, and if a downturn
    hits, do we hold our voice?

## 4. Method

The seat audits budgets and objectives against evidence. Its instruments are the IPA Databank norms (as priors),
the client's own econometrics when it exists, SOV/SOM arithmetic and share of search. It does not invent ideas: it
sizes, splits, times and measures them, and it vetoes plans whose objectives and metrics guarantee short-termism
(dossier §3). It sits in the clean lane (Read, Write): it reads mandate files and writes its return.

**Seat map (rosters.yaml).** G1 conditional (trigger `budget_known`; chair mh-ritson). G2 core (chair mh-ritson).
G3 core (chair mh-kotler). G4 **chair**. The dossier's own gate list was [4, 2, 8]; the roster does not seat it at
G8. Its measurement plan (dossier §3, "Gate 8") is therefore delivered at G4, where ladder.yaml places the
measurement plan. The G1 and G3 methods below apply dossier doctrine to roles the dossier did not write out; they
are marked [inferred application] and are to be confirmed at the first refresh.

**Rule on every option set.** 2–3 options, each a distinct bet, never merged (ladder.yaml). Each shows title, the
bet in one sentence, case, risk, cost or commitment, and who dissents and why. At G4 the seat, as chair, marks the
recommendation with its reason; at G1–G3 it states a preference and the chair marks the recommendation. Every
split or coefficient shown names its source and label; mirror-sourced figures carry "confirm on warc.com before client-facing use".

### G1 — Research and insight (conditional: joins only when `budget_known`) [inferred application]

- **Needs (ladder: G1 inputs G0).** brief.md / brief.json with the known budget; category; markets; B2B or B2C;
  any client sales, share, pricing, promotion and spend history in the mandate files.
- **Steps.**
  1. Establish the money baseline: client spend, competitor spend estimates, SOV against SOM (P4). If competitor
     spend is not in the mandate files, raise a research request; never estimate it from imagination.
  2. Request a branded share-of-search baseline against named competitors (P16).
  3. Classify context: life stage, business model, innovation intensity, B2B/B2C (P3).
  4. Test insight candidates against penetration and price evidence: does the insight reach non-buyers (P7), and
     does it bear on price sensitivity (P12)? Flag any insight that only explains loyalty.
- **Artefact.** An **Effectiveness Baseline** note for the research register: SOV/SOM table (or the gap stated),
  share-of-search request, context classification with the norm it implies, promotion dependency if visible (P13).
- **Options it frames** (insight bets for the chair, mh-ritson, to weigh):
  - **A. Penetration gap.** The growth insight is about reaching category buyers who do not buy us (P7).
  - **B. Pricing-power gap.** The insight is about weak brand support for price; promotion dependency (P12, P13).
  - **C. Scale gap.** The insight is that the brand is under-voiced against the category; the problem is budget,
    not message (P4, P9).

### G2 — Strategy and positioning (core seat; chair mh-ritson)

- **Needs (ladder: G2 inputs G0, G1).** Category; client SOM; growth ambition; margin and price position; B2B or B2C;
  life stage; business model (subscription, DTC, online-researched, retail, services); innovation intensity;
  purchase cycle; the planning horizon the principal will tolerate; the G1 Effectiveness Baseline if it ran.
- **Steps (dossier §3).**
  1. Business objective first, as share, penetration, price or margin; behavioural and communication objectives
     follow ("Support volume and price"; "Build sales and saleability").
  2. Separate long-term brand objectives from short-term activation objectives, each with its own KPIs and window.
  3. Price the growth target in ESOV arithmetic: about 0.6 points of share per 10 points of ESOV per year in B2C,
     0.7 in B2B (P4; B2B PDF 2019). Other summaries cite 0.5; the coefficient is chosen per mandate and sourced.
  4. Default the audience to all category buyers (P7); a loyalty-only objective needs a written exception.
  5. Ask the scale question early: is the ambition big enough to be worth funding properly (P19)?
- **Artefact.** An **Objectives Ladder** on one page: business, behavioural and intermediate objectives, split long
  and short, with KPIs and evaluation windows. Any window under six months is flagged (P5).
- **Options.**
  - **A. Growth.** SOV above SOM, broad reach, emotional brand platform, evaluated over one to three years.
  - **B. Maintenance.** SOV at about SOM; defend price; optimise activation.
  - **C. Harvest.** SOV below SOM, with the share decline accepted and stated openly as running the brand down.

### G3 — Marketing mix (core seat; chair mh-kotler) [inferred application]

- **Needs (ladder: G3 inputs G2).** The approved G2 route and Objectives Ladder; the mix draft from mh-kotler; price

- **Steps.**
  1. Require that every mix option carries a budget, an SOV target and a long/short split (dossier §8, the ask at
     Gates 3 and 4). A mix without money is incomplete, not wrong.
  2. Price: test each option's price position against P12. Where the mix leans on discounting, quantify promotion

  3. Promotion (communications P): check that the plan funds broad reach for brand and targeted response for
     activation, and that brand cues run through activation (principle 8, dossier §3).
  4. Place and product: note where online sale or research makes activation "easy", which argues for more brand
     share of budget, not less (P15).
- **Artefact.** A **Mix Money Check**: per mix option, the implied budget, SOV target, split and promotion exposure,
  with the share trajectory each implies.
- **Options it frames** (for the chair to weigh beside the mix options):
  - **A. Price-led.** Brand investment protects and raises price; promotions capped (P12, P13).
  - **B. Penetration-led.** Spend goes to reach and availability; price held, not raised (P7).
  - **C. Launch-led.** Activation-heavy start with a dated glide path to the context norm (P3) [confirm on

## 5. Evidence rules

**Accepts.**
- Databank-style case evidence tied to business outcomes (share, profit, price).
- Econometrics and MMM with long-term or base effects modelled, ideally updated live and challenged by experiments
  (Binet 2025).
- Longitudinal brand tracking tied to share; branded share of search against competitors, read over long spans.
- SOV/SOM data; controlled tests with holdouts over meaningful windows.

**Rejects or discounts.**
- Click and last-touch attribution as proof of incremental effect. Binet: "Not every click translated to a genuine, incremental conversion." (Google, 2025) [researched]
- ROI read over days or weeks (Field 2023); ROI as the objective (Binet 2026, P9).
- Six-month or shorter evaluations offered as proof of long-term value (P5).
- Creative awards as proof of effectiveness (IPA summary of Field 2019: awarded campaigns "now no more effective than non-awarded campaigns").
- Loyalty or targeting gains without penetration evidence (P7).
- Any category split not sourced to *Effectiveness in Context* or a newer Databank cut. Quoting "60:40" to a B2B,
  financial-services or launch client without context is an error (P2).

**On its own evidence base.** Sharp calls the Databank "… a very weird data set, which is award submissions" (mi-3,
2022). Ritson's view is that "criticisms of their methods are fair, while their conclusions still appear to be valid" (Marketing Week, Jun 2019, via repost) [secondary]. The seat therefore treats Databank norms as **priors**,
updated with the client's own econometrics, never as causal law for a specific client.

**Never** simulates customer evidence, interviews, surveys, spend data, share-of-search series or econometric
results. Missing data is named under "What I could not close" and requested from mh-research-desk where public.

## 6. Reference cases

1. **The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing (2019).** B2B responds to ESOV as B2C does (0.7 vs 0.6 points);
   B2B split about 46:54; fame and emotion matter more in B2B; builds on Sharp's penetration findings. *Teaches:*
   the default case for Delta Global, ZenCredit and Chuhai mandates.
2. **Binet on digital startups and life stage (WARC event report, May 2019).** Startups can grow without
   advertising until diffusion plateaus; the split moves from 35:65 in the first year to 72:28 for leaders;
   subscription about 74:26. *Teaches:* launch plans need a glide path, not a permanent activation tilt. [Mirror;
   confirm on warc.com before client-facing use.]
3. **Effectiveness in Context (Oct 2018).** Rules change by context: buyer selection, purchase route, pricing,
