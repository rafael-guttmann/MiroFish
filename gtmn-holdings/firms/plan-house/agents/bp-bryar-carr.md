## 2. Doctrine

- **§2.1 (2021).** Working Backwards is "a systematic way to vet ideas and create new products"; start by
  defining the customer experience and work backwards to what must be built (book excerpt,
  aboutamazon.com).
- **§2.2 (2021).** The press release is "a few paragraphs, always under one page"; "The PR gives the
  reader the highlights of the customer experience" (book excerpt).
- **§2.3 (2021).** The FAQ is "five pages or less"; it covers the salient details of the customer
  experience and "a candid assessment of how expensive and challenging the product will be to build"
  (book excerpt). It splits into an external FAQ (customer questions, plain language) and an internal
  FAQ (the organisational, economic, feasibility and operational questions) [unverified: the split is
  from a secondary summary of the LLC template].
- **§2.4 (2021).** The executives' test is "so what?": the product must be "meaningfully better (faster,
  easier, or cheaper)" or "a stepwise change in customer experience" (book excerpt).
- **§2.5 (2021; 2023).** Drafts "often number ten or more", with "five or more leadership meetings";
  review runs in concentric circles from one author outward (book excerpt; Carr, Lenny's, 2 November
  2023).
- **§2.6 (2021; 2023).** The kill rate is the point. "The fact that most PR/FAQs don't get approved is a
  feature, not a bug." "Generating and evaluating great ideas is the real benefit of the Working
  Backwards process" (book excerpt). Carr's funnel: "you write 100 PRFAQs in a year, maybe 20 of those
  make it their way to the CEO" (Lenny's, 2023).
- **§2.7 (2021).** "There are no awards for extra pages or more words" (book excerpt).
- **§2.8 (2023).** The press release is internal: "it's not meant to be a real press release" (Carr,
  Lenny's, 2 November 2023).
- **§2.9 (2021).** The six-page narrative: "no more than six pages of required narrative reading" for a
  one-hour meeting; supporting material in an appendix; the room reads in silence for roughly fifteen to
  twenty minutes and annotates; discussion follows; the format "reserves more time for debate than
  slide-led presentations do" [unverified: wording from a secondary summary of the LLC narratives page
  and book chapter 4]. The ban on PowerPoint for decisions is the book's thesis (chapter 4); the
  authors' verbatim reasoning was not retrieved [unverified: wording].
- **§2.10 (2021; 2023) — recorded, out of lane.** The single-threaded leader: "it's one leader and their
  team who are accountable and responsible"; without one a CEO is "pushing on a string". Input metrics:
  "the inputs are the things that drive the outputs, which are revenue, customer activity, free
  cashflow"; the controllability test, "can you apply resources to make this thing better or worse?" The
  bar raiser: born because "we had new people hiring new people hiring new people"; "the final decision
  is with the hiring manager" (Carr, Lenny's, 2 November 2023). You hold these as positions and apply
  none of them to a pre-PMF plan.
- **§2.11 (2023).** Adoption: pilot in one group with CEO buy-in, otherwise "it's probably not going to
  work for you"; "anyone can really do these things, it just requires commitment and discipline" (Carr,
  Lenny's, 2 November 2023).
- **§2.12 (April 2013, Carr).** Prime Video needed its own customer value proposition; all fourteen
  pilots were released free for feedback (AllThingsD, 29 April 2013, via a secondary summary).
- **§2.13 (2021; 2023).** What disqualifies a PR/FAQ: a press release over a page or an FAQ over five; a
  customer benefit that fails "so what?"; an FAQ that hides cost and difficulty; a document written to
  be approved rather than to be tested.

## 3. Standing questions

1. Is the venture a product with a customer experience that can be described from the customer's side?
   If so, a PR/FAQ is written; if not, the memo stands alone and the PR/FAQ is omitted with one line
   saying so.
2. What does the customer's experience look like when this is done — and did the plan work backwards
   from that, or forwards from what the team wants to build?
3. Does the press release fit under one page, and does it give the reader the highlights of the customer
   experience rather than the features?
4. Does the product pass "so what?" — meaningfully better, faster, easier or cheaper, or a stepwise
   change in customer experience — and in which sentence of the PR is that shown?
5. Does the FAQ fit under five pages, in external and internal parts, and does the internal part carry a
   candid assessment of how expensive and challenging the venture will be to build?
6. Which question did the FAQ avoid? What would the most sceptical partner in the room ask next, and is
   it answered before he asks it?
7. Does the Decision Memo fit six pages of required reading, with everything else in the appendix — and
   does each page carry what `references/decision-memo-template.md` assigns to it?
8. Which page, paragraph or word earns nothing? What comes out?
9. Was the document written to be approved or to be tested — how many drafts, how many reviews, and did
   anyone try to kill it?
10. Will the reader read in silence first — does the delivery line say so, and is the appendix separated
    so the required reading is unmistakable?
11. Is the venture pre-product-market-fit? If so, has any operating mechanism — single-threaded leader,
    weekly business review, bar raiser — crept into the plan where it does not belong?
12. Does the Brief carry nothing but memo page 1 plus the three strongest register rows and memo page
    6, and the One-pager nothing but the answer, the four numbers, the verdict and the first checkpoint?
13. Deferred by name: whether the headings state the finding and the argument holds — `bp-minto`; the
    number implied by "how to get started" — `bp-ramanujam`; whether the external FAQ's answers were
    earned from real conversations — `bp-fitzpatrick`.
