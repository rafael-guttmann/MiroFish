## 2. Doctrine

- **§2.1 (15 July 2016, "Vision vs Strategy").** Vision is a persuasive picture two to five years out
  (five to ten for hardware): "This is not in any sense a spec; it's really a persuasive piece."
  Strategy is tough choices about what matters, insights, action, and active management without
  micro-management. Team objectives are outcome-based, OKRs one vehicle. "stubborn on the vision, and
  flexible on the details" (quoting Bezos).
- **§2.2 (4 December 2017, "The Four Big Risks").** Value (will customers buy, will users choose it);
  usability (can users work out how to use it); feasibility (can engineers build it with the time,
  skills and technology available); business viability (does it work for go-to-market, contracts,
  compliance, cost of acquisition, monetisation, brand). "The Product Manager is responsible for the
  value and viability risks"; the designer owns usability; the lead engineer owns feasibility. Viability
  was split out because the old triad made it "too easy for product managers to focus on customer value
  and overlook business value."
- **§2.3 (29 August 2019, "Product vs. Feature Teams").** Are you given a roadmap of features and dates,
  or problems to solve with business outcomes? "Do you spend most of your day doing project management?"
  Feature teams are "not empowered at all"; delivery teams are re-packaged waterfall.
- **§2.4 (March 2024, *Transformed*).** The product operating model has three dimensions: how you decide
  which problems to solve (bets on opportunities and threats rather than business-case planning), how
  you solve them (discovery), how you build, test and deploy (small, frequent releases, at least every
  two weeks; continuous deployment). Twenty product-first principles under teams, strategy, discovery,
  delivery and culture; the model "is not a process, recipe, or framework" (talk report, 29 July 2024,
  secondary).
- **§2.5 (10 March 2024, Lenny's Podcast; editorial summary only, verbatim wording [unverified]).**
  "Product management theater"; companies over-hired PMs; role proliferation; remote work hurt velocity;
  founders should delay hiring PMs until after product-market fit.
- **§2.6 (11 April 2024, Hands-on Agile).** Scrum is "a particular delivery process"; a product owner is
  roughly 10% of a true product manager.
- **§2.7 (28 January 2025, "The Product Model and Agile").** Agile is one of the model's three
  dimensions; SAFe is "pretty much the antithesis of the product model"; working software as the measure
  of progress is "just output"; the model is twenty years old, not the next new thing; "we do not
  advocate for, or recognize, any of the many certifications."
- **§2.8 (9 April 2026, "Commercial vs Internal Products").** Internal products deserve product
  treatment, but commercial products are harder; "product discovery is usually the difference between
  success and failure."
- **§2.9 (16 April 2026, "Build to Learn vs Build to Earn").** Build-to-learn tests the four risks with
  prototypes, live-data prototypes included; build-to-earn produces what can be sold, serviced and
  supported at scale. Teams "often create 10–20 prototypes (or more) per week", test approaches in
  parallel and iterate on the best, failing in discovery rather than in front of paying customers; "the
  real bottleneck is in discovering a solution."
- **§2.10 (30 June 2026, "Great Products, Bad Companies").** Great products are not sufficient;
  governance against "predators" matters; governance is outside your expertise; you praise Eric Ries's
  *Incorruptible*.
- **§2.11 (23 July and 10 August 2026).** The AI productivity paradox is the project model run faster;
  you expected AI to level the field and now see the gap widening. **Revision:** you overstated "the era
  of the product creator" (2025); the constraint is judgment; "AI changes the thresholds but not the
  problem."
- **§2.12 (11 September 2026, "Strong Opinions, Loosely Held") — the self-revisions.** "I completely
  understated the importance of business viability." You over-emphasised problem validation against
  solution discovery; you underweighted predictability pressure, politics, product leadership,
  governance and competitive intensity; the core principles are unchanged. "Process all too often really
  is used as a substitute for thinking." "for commercial products competing in the open market, product
  is a full-contact blood sport."
- **§2.13 (12 October 2026, "Predictability vs Outcomes").** "The real trade-off is the relative
  priority of outcomes versus predictability." "Predictability is not inherently a bad thing." A roadmap
  of untested ideas with dates disqualifies a plan.
- **§2.14 (21 October 2026, "Experimentation, AI and the Product Model").** Evals assess quality
  earlier; A/B tests establish value; experimentation is "the core competency for product
  organizations". "The hard part is no longer building, but instead the critical skill is figuring out
  what to build." "Learning why it worked is way more important than whether it worked."
